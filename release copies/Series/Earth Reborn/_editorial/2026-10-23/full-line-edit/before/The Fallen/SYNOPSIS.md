# The Fallen
## Earth Reborn Book One

**Author:** Raymond Bayly  
**Structure:** Prologue and 16 chapters

## Reader-facing description

Captain Marcus Gavin escaped a dying Earth aboard the ship meant to preserve humanity. He expected to wake when Earth was safe.

Instead, he falls alone into a changed landscape, carrying an engineer’s memories and equipment that can no longer explain what surrounds him. Orah offers him a place in that world. For a time, a home and honest work seem possible.

But the people who rule this land recognize power before they recognize mercy. As Marcus discovers what he can do, he draws the attention of forces that will not leave him in peace.

To survive, he must learn the laws of a world that has forgotten his own—and decide what kind of man he will be when those laws fail the people he loves.

## Editorial synopsis — spoilers

Humanity escapes the alien weapon’s devastation aboard the ARC. Its twenty thousand passengers sleep near Mars before the ship returns and releases pods to Earth. Some pods remain aloft much longer. Marcus is one of these late arrivals.

An Army Corps of Engineers officer whose work included irrigation in Africa, Marcus is a builder with military training. His early encounters with the changed world lead him to Orah and the possibility of a settled life. The story also follows Corliss, Rogan, and Nero, revealing the institutions and ambitions that surround this apparent refuge.

Orah’s murder destroys that refuge. Marcus’s uncontrolled power kills her attackers, leaving him shaken by both grief and what he has done. Maelen becomes his guide to the gift and the world’s magical traditions.

Their conflict with the Empress’s servants grows into open resistance. In Necrosis, they help a town reject the Black Queen’s authority, but the victory brings news that the wider agreement has broken down. The closing chapters follow Nero’s search for sanctuary after the coastal slaughter and the Wardens’ northern alliance.

The book ends with the conflict widening, rather than with a decisive victory over a warlord named Aurtus.

## Continuity anchors

The ARC is a spacecraft, not an underground stasis installation. The prologue places the end of the First Age at the close of the twenty-second century. Marcus’s engineering experience and limited combat experience should govern his early tactics. Corliss uses she/her pronouns in the narrative. Honor is explicitly named as the first of Aurtus’s four tenets; a complete list should not be supplied without supporting text.
