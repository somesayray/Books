# Mortis
## Earth Reborn prequel

**Author:** Raymond Bayly  
**Form:** Short prequel; 26 chapters

## Reader-facing description

A prince is dead behind a locked door, and the wounds on his body belong to no weapon Lord Speaker Darius Spencer knows.

As the court demands vengeance, Darius follows the evidence beneath Oakhaven with the royal mortician Murat and the scholar Arianna. What waits below the city is older than the kingdom: a machine built to preserve order when human government failed.

Above them, the Inquisition prepares to impose an order of its own. Darius must decide whom he can trust before the investigation becomes a civil war—and before the cure for the kingdom’s weakness becomes a power no one can restrain.

## Editorial synopsis — spoilers

Prince Valen’s death draws Darius, Murat, and Arianna into an investigation of Precursor technology beneath Oakhaven. The violence exposes both the royal court’s fragility and the ambitions of High Inquisitor Malakor. As religious repression spreads, the investigators reach Project Mortis, a system capable of reshaping living bodies and controlling mechanical warriors.

Arianna reveals that her relationship to the machine is personal: she claims descent from its programmer and intends to become its human interface. Her communion with Mortis gives her the means to defeat the Inquisition and claim the throne. The order she establishes brings material benefits, but it also places her subjects under invasive biological control.

Darius becomes the moral counterweight to her power. Offered life extension, he refuses it. His pact is to serve as the people’s voice for the remainder of his mortal life, not to become an eternal Speaker.

The final chapter places an aged Darius beside the Black Queen as she detects a returning sleeper and names Marcus. This links the prequel to the main narrative, but the Queen’s origin and the geographic details of that link still need reconciliation with the trilogy.

## Continuity status

Arianna’s descent from a First Age programmer and transformation through Mortis are not yet reconciled with Victoria/Eleneaor’s account of her own ancient military career in The Black Queen. Neither an alias nor inherited memory should be assumed without a scene establishing it. The manuscript also places Marcus’s re-entry over the southern ice sheets; confirm its relationship to his landing before treating this as a settled series chronology.

At approximately 16,000 words, this is substantially shorter than the other books. Its current form should be described accurately in publication materials.
