## Findings requiring structural or canon decisions

### 1. Book Three is assembled from earlier material

The comparison was run against the pre-edit snapshots. The Dawn of Subjugation is 98.27% similar to The Fallen chapter 15; Northern Dominance is 98.65% similar to its chapter 16. All twenty remaining numbered chapters match The Black Queen at 98.88–99.72% word-sequence similarity. Holy Ruler is 82.10% similar and depicts the same outcome: Nero’s regency, Corliss’s leadership, and Marcus’s departure.

These percentages measure sequence similarity, not the percentage of words that are original. Chapter labels and trailing publication markers were excluded. The complete comparison is in [chapter-overlap.json](./_editorial/2026-10-23/chapter-overlap.json).

The original Draconis/Fall of the Empire.docx retrieved through Author MCP begins with a Black Queen title page, a placeholder prologue, and The Dawn of Subjugation. It ends with the same northward departure. Black Queen-2021.docx begins In Search Of and likewise ends with the departure. Thus the inspected sources do not establish a separate sequel hidden behind the release conversion. This does not establish that no other source exists elsewhere.

**Refinement prepared:** the candidate division preserves The Fallen, ends Book Two after Check and Mate (Part III), and transfers the remaining eight chapters and epilogue to Book Three. It yields approximately 151,454 / 62,831 / 49,962 words. Every numbered chapter in the revised Book Two appears exactly once across candidate Books Two and Three, with its body unchanged. The candidate third book starts Si Vis Pacem, Para Bellum and owns the final campaign.

**Editorial cost:** Book Two acquires a violent, Nero-centered ending and needs a closing beat for Marcus. Book Three needs a brief reorientation at its opening. This is a possible reconstruction of the existing story, not a newly written sequel. If Book Two’s current ending must remain, a distinct Book Three needs new narrative material.

### 2. The added recaps contradicted the ARC premise

The Fallen’s prologue establishes an alien weapon, an ARC spacecraft, twenty thousand sleeping passengers, a wait near Mars, and staggered pod returns. Its opening identifies Marcus as an engineer who worked on irrigation in Africa and has limited combat experience. The earlier later-book recaps and synopses substituted underground vaults, nuclear/tectonic catastrophe, a twenty-first-century origin, and claims about genetic keys and orbital arsenals.

**Applied:** rewrote the trilogy recaps and supporting copy around supported events. The Fallen’s prologue now presents the same ship-based premise more clearly and concisely. Marcus remains an engineer with military training. Avoid turning him into an experienced combat specialist simply to make an action scene convenient; let planning, observation, improvisation, and mistakes express his background.

### 3. The Queen’s identities remain unreconciled

Mortis presents Arianna as a descendant of the Mortis programmer who merges with the machine. The Fallen names Empress Victoria Augustus. In The Black Queen’s final confrontation, the Empress identifies herself as Eleneaor and claims a personal history involving Tokimada and Zathris nearly a thousand years earlier.

Aliases alone do not reconcile descent, embodiment, memory, and age. The text needs an explicit bridge if these are one person: for example, a deliberate account of inherited memory or an unreliable identity claim. Those would be new story decisions. None was silently added in this pass. Choose the intended explanation before refining clues in Mortis, or its reveal may promise a different history from the trilogy’s payoff.

### 4. The prequel’s timeline needed local repair

Darius is fifty-two in chapter one, but chapter two described fifty years of life. A later passage gave him fifty years of war and statecraft. Chapter 25 makes him fifty-four; the final chapter then said thirty years passed while calling him eighty-two.

**Applied:** fifty-two in the earlier age reference; decades of war and statecraft; the final chapter is now anchored to the thirtieth year after Valen’s death. This keeps his final age consistent without inventing a new lifespan. The interval before the pact can still be made more explicit during a full revision.

The final scene’s southern ice-sheet re-entry report, the Queen’s advance knowledge of Marcus’s name, and the relationship between Oakhaven and the trilogy’s imperial geography still need a canon check. They are recorded as questions rather than rewritten as facts.

### 5. Religious and character summaries had drifted from the text

The Fallen’s chapter-one epigraph explicitly calls Honor the first tenet. The earlier recaps instead supplied Life, Order, Balance, and Memory as a complete list. That unsupported list has been removed from the edited recaps and synopses. Establish all four from authoritative narrative evidence before adding a series glossary.

Corliss uses she/her pronouns in the narrative. Supporting copy should preserve that. The existing source also varies Korlin/Kolrim and includes other similar names; unlike an obvious misspelling of Marcus, those require a character-register decision before a global replacement. This pass did not collapse them indiscriminately.

## Craft assessment

### Mortis

The investigation has a strong sequence of escalating discoveries: the body, the forbidden technology, the political emergency, and the cost of accepting the machine’s protection. Darius’s refusal of immortality gives the final pact moral weight and makes him more than a witness to Arianna’s rise.

At approximately 16,000 words across 26 chapters, the book is a short prequel, not comparable in scale to the main volumes. Later chapters compress major political changes into summary. If expanding, dramatize one consequence of the blood-binding policy through a person Darius must help; this would test his pact rather than merely explain its importance. If keeping the short form, present it accurately and retain the sharp investigation-to-coronation progression.

The dark/steel/cold imagery establishes a consistent atmosphere, but some passages repeat both the image and its explanation. Let a concrete action carry the implication. The ending’s summary of decades of prosperity is particularly suited to selective compression balanced by one specific cost.

### The Fallen

The most distinctive element is a capable builder discovering that competence does not spare him grief or responsibility. Orah’s death and Marcus’s uncontrolled retaliation provide the clearest rupture in the sampled narrative. Preserve his shock at his own violence; treating it as a simple power upgrade would weaken the character.

Several chapters are exceptionally long: Marcus and Orah exceed 15,000 words each, and Maelen exceeds 17,000. Inspect changes of location, time, immediate objective, or viewpoint for scene breaks. Do not divide mechanically at equal word counts. The opening repeatedly explains disorientation after showing it; the revised prologue demonstrates a more economical approach without altering the premise.

Orah’s role warrants particular care in a developmental pass. Ensure her wants, decisions, and relationships matter before the killing, so the loss remains the death of a person rather than solely a device to unlock Marcus’s power.

### The Black Queen

Rogan’s opening establishes his cruelty immediately. The refined entrance separates the accusation, the men’s predicament, and his rationalization into readable beats. The wider chapter still needs dialogue punctuation and sentence-boundary work; the targeted opening edit does not stand in for that full pass.

The stronger political tension lies in the incompatible aims of the coalition: defeating the Empress does not mean agreeing about who may rule or whether mages may live freely. Nero’s attack on the creature at the end of Check and Mate (Part III) makes that conflict personal. If using it as Book Two’s ending, give Marcus a final consequential choice as well, rather than leaving the principal viewpoint absent from the volume’s last turn.

### Fall of the Empire

In the proposed division, the final campaign has an identifiable function: preparing power, choosing obligations, fighting the regime, and discovering the limits of victory. The Empress’s escape and the Church’s stewardship make the ending morally unsettled. That can be effective if the book clearly resolves its principal conflict while presenting those consequences as the next problem.

The Empress’s warning about the threat across the waters should complicate the reader’s judgment without retrospectively making her atrocities necessary. Marcus’s departure also needs emotional continuity with his friends’ losses and the new government’s risk. The existing farewell contains that material but still has long, loosely joined sentences around the revised lines.

## Copyediting completed and remaining

The manuscript log contains 82 edit records covering 250 occurrences. An occurrence may be one word, one political title, or one replaced passage; this is not a count of 250 independently reviewed scenes or sentences.

Applied fixes include Lord Reagent → Lord Regent, Emporer → Emperor, Macrcus/Marcsus → Marcus, selected spelling errors, and context-specific repairs such as a harness around a waist, cooking meat, and shutting down a tower. Broad replacement of ambiguous terms such as there/their, reign/rain, and loosing/losing was avoided. The old notes’ claim of comprehensive cleanup was not borne out by the inspected text.

Remaining work includes dialogue punctuation, comma splices, thought attribution, possessives, duplicated explanations, inconsistent capitalization, and residual draft labels. Examples visible in the candidate campaign include “Sic Pac Parabellum” beneath the corrected Latin chapter title and “Forced in Either” beneath Forced Choices. They should be assessed against the source, then removed or resolved during the full line edit.

## Verification and next decision

All recorded manuscript replacements replay exactly from the saved snapshots to the edited files. Chapter headings and their order remain unchanged in the four release manuscripts. The proposal’s repartition check confirms that it neither loses nor duplicates Book Two’s numbered narrative. The report and manifests distinguish revised Markdown from the unchanged binary formats.

The next decision is whether to adopt the proposed division or keep Book Two intact and develop a new sequel. After that, reconcile the Queen’s history, complete the line edit on the chosen masters, rebuild the synopses and binary editions from those masters, and inspect the exported layouts. A claim that the trilogy is publication-ready would be premature before those steps.
