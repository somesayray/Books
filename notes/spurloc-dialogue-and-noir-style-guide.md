# Style Guide: Spurloc Cyber-Noir & Dialogue Pacing

## Overview
A comprehensive craft guide detailing voice, tone, atmospheric styling, and dialogue rhythm for the **Martin Spurloc** detective novels and short stories.

---

## 1. The Noir Narrative Voice

### First-Person Cynicism
- **POV**: First-person past tense ("I watched the rain smear across the synth-glass...").
- **Tone**: World-weary, observant, bone-dry humor, and deeply cynical about corporate institutions and the wealthy elite.
- **Sensory Details**:
  - The constant hum of ozone from neon signs and high-voltage MagLev lines.
  - The metallic smell of rain mixed with cheap synth-coffee and sulfur exhaust from the lower vents.
  - The subtle whir of servomotors and cooling fans inside Spurloc’s left titanium shoulder joint when the temperature drops.

---

## 2. Dynamic Duo: Spurloc & Elvis Dialogue Rhythm

### Contrasting Personalities
- **Martin Spurloc**: Understated, gruff, economical with words. Never raises his voice; lets kinetic threats and cocked hammers do the intimidation.
- **Elvis (AI)**: Flamboyant, talkative, charming, steeped in 1950s rock-and-roll idioms, Southern cadences, and pop-culture non-sequiturs, but with lightning-fast decryption skills.

### Sample Dialogue Exchanges

```text
Spurloc glanced down at the shattered glass on the balcony, then touched the scorch mark on the doorframe. The metal was still warm.

"Elvis, give me a scan on this residue. Tell me our shooter didn't use a standard plasma emitter."

A golden holographic shimmer resolved above Spurloc’s titanium forearm, projecting a miniature silhouette leaning against an imaginary mic stand.

"Well, bless my soul, boss man," Elvis drawled, his synthesized Southern voice cutting through the distant rumble of the Metroplex sirens. "That ain't plasma. That's a focused ionized slug from a Kurosawa pulse-rifle. Military grade. Expensive. The kind of toy that says 'I have private jet money and zero respect for municipal law.'"

"How many registered in this district?"

"Officially? Zero. Unofficially? The CEO of Danforth Heavy Industries had six shipped to his penthouse last Tuesday. Want me to slice into their guest registry, or are we gonna do this the hard way?"

Spurloc racked the slide on his kinetic sidearm, his cybernetic eye flashing crimson as the tactical HUD locked onto the stairwell door.

"You know me, Elvis. I don't know any easy ways."

"That's what I was afraid of, Martin. Put some pomade in your hair, we've got company."
```

---

## 3. Scene Construction Rules

1. **The Investigation Rule**: Every scene must yield a clue or a reversal. No passive talking-heads scenes—dialogue should occur while searching a ransacked apartment, escaping an ambush, or tailing a suspect through crowded synth-markets.
2. **The Magic-Tech Rule**: Keep Spurloc's warlock abilities costly. Magic isn't effortless Harry Potter wand-waving; it draws from biological stamina and produces painful feedback in his cybernetic neural connections if overused.
3. **The Pacing Escalation**:
   - Chapter opening: Atmospheric observation and detective deduction.
   - Middle: Confrontation, interrogation, or digital breach.
   - Chapter ending: Sudden betrayal, forensic revelation, or tactical explosion.
